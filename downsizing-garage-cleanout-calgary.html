Where Is Everything Going?

A garage holds two kinds of things: the ones a household uses every week, and the ones it stopped using years ago but never had a reason to move. Downsizing forces a decision on both. The new home sets the limit, and each kind of home sets a different one. Ask the building or the residence these questions before anyone starts sorting, because the answers rule things in or out faster than any amount of deciding:

The Gas Cans, the Mower and the Snowblower

Anything with fuel in it needs a plan, because movers and most donation centres won't take it as it is, and a condo building's bylaws decide whether it can sit in a locker or a parking stall. Ask the property manager before moving day, not after the truck arrives.

Here is where each one goes in Calgary if it isn't moving:

  • Gas cans and leftover gasoline or diesel. The City takes packaged gasoline and diesel and fuel containers free at its household hazardous waste drop-offs at designated fire halls, such as Fire Station #20 at 2800 Peacekeepers Way SW, open 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. daily. The City's limit is 20 litres of household hazardous waste per visit. If your parent wants to keep the jerry can, the gas has to be emptied at a City Eco Centre at a landfill instead, because the fire halls keep the container.
  • Barbecue and patio heater propane. Free at the same drop-offs for cylinders under 30 lb. If the barbecue is going to a condo balcony, check whether the building allows propane at all.
  • The lawnmower, trimmer and snowblower. A working machine is worth more to a neighbour or a family member than to the landfill. Empty the fuel first. A buyer of the house sometimes wants the snowblower, so it can be offered through the realtor before anything else is decided.
  • Car batteries and the battery charger. Lead-acid vehicle batteries go free to the hazardous waste drop-off.
  • The second car, the trailer, the boat. We clear around them. Selling a vehicle is a job for your family or a dealer.

If a gas can, a propane tank or a box of shotgun shells turns up in the rafters, we set it aside and tell you before anything leaves. Our estate cleanout page lists what we stop for, including firearms.

Giving Things Away While Your Parent Can Still Say Who Gets Them

A downsizing garage is different from an estate garage in one way that matters to families: the person who owns everything is alive and can decide. That's easier when the decisions are small and concrete. "Which of these drills does Mark want?" gets an answer. "What should happen to the tools?" usually gets "I'll think about it."

If a Move Manager or Estate Sale Company Is Already Involved

Calgary has senior move managers who pack, plan the floor layout of the new suite and run moving day, and estate sale companies that hold a sale in the house before it empties. The garage still needs someone to clear what doesn't move and doesn't sell. That's where we fit, in this order:

  1. Family picks happen first, before anything is priced for a sale.
  2. The sale goes next, if there is one. Tools, a mower and a workbench can sell at a garage or estate sale, so let the sale company see the garage before we do.
  3. Moving day follows. The move manager and the movers take what's going to the new home.
  4. We come after the truck leaves. We clear what didn't sell and wasn't wanted, route each stream to the right place, and sweep the floor.

If you already have a move manager, give them our number and we'll work to their schedule. If the house is going to market and the garage has to be empty for listing photos, our cleanout before selling page covers that timeline instead.

Short Visits, With Your Parent There

A garage that took forty years to fill doesn't need to be decided in one afternoon, and a parent of eighty shouldn't be asked to. If your parent wants to be there while we sort, we can split the job:

  • A sorting visit with your parent in a chair in the doorway, out of the cold. We bring things to them. They say keep, family, donate or go. We stop when they're tired.
  • A hauling visit after the move, with nobody needing to be there. We take what was marked to go, and what's left after family has collected.

Two visits cost more than one, and we'll show you both prices from the same photos so you can choose. If your parent has already moved or doesn't want to be part of it, one visit with a family member on the phone works too. Moving Day Comes Before Possession Day

If your parent moves out before the buyer takes the keys, the garage cleanout fits in that gap. Book it for after the movers and before the final walk-through, and leave a day of margin for the things family said they'd collect and didn't.

In a Calgary winter, give that margin more room. The City doesn't plow back lanes, so a detached garage on a lane in Lakeview, Britannia or Mount Royal can be harder to reach after a heavy snowfall. If you were planning a bin instead of a crew, a bin in the lane needs a City street use permit at $31.50 a day, applied for at least five business days ahead. Our bin or crew page has the full comparison. If the date moves at short notice, a same-day cleanout is sometimes possible.

After the garage is empty, the buyer will see the floor. Twenty winters of road salt and oil drips show up on bare concrete, and garage floor cleaning can follow the same week. For walls, door tracks and windows, see garage cleaning in Calgary.

What Changes the Quote

Downsizing garages take longer per square foot than other cleanouts because more of the job is sorting. These are the things that move the price, so you can look at your parent's garage and guess which way it'll go:

  • How much is being kept or set aside. Keeping things takes longer than hauling them, because each kept item gets moved, marked and left where someone can find it.
  • How many places the contents have to go. Hazardous waste, the landfill, tires and electronics kept separate, donation, and family. A garage with all five takes more trips than one with two.
  • Heavy and awkward items. A chest freezer (with its $17 refrigerant fee at the landfill), a cast-iron table saw, an anvil, a bolted-down workbench, a gun safe.
  • One visit or two. The sorting visit with your parent is its own block of time.
  • Access. A front driveway is faster than a long carry from a lane garage or a basement workshop.

Questions From Families Downsizing in Calgary

How much does a downsizing garage cleanout cost in Calgary?

Our full cleanouts with junk removal start at $399, and we quote each garage from photos before anything is booked. A downsizing garage usually costs more than a plain cleanout of the same size because more of the job is sorting: keeping what moves apart from what goes to family, what gets donated and what gets thrown out. The things that move the price are volume, how many separate drop-offs the contents need (hazardous waste, the landfill, donation), heavy items such as a chest freezer or a workbench, and whether the work happens in one visit or is split around the moving date. The list above goes through each one.

What should you get rid of first when downsizing?

In the garage, start with the things the next home can't take at all. If the move is to a condo or a seniors' residence, that is usually the gas lawnmower, the snowblower, the gas cans, the propane cylinders and the long ladders. They take the most room, and deciding them first shows how much space is left for everything else. Then deal with the paint shelf and the chemicals, which go to a free City household hazardous waste drop-off. Sentimental boxes come last, when the deadline is clearer and family can help.

Who will pick up donations for free in Calgary?

Habitat for Humanity's ReStores in Calgary offer free pick-up and tax receipts for qualifying donations, and they accept tools, hardware and renovation materials. Call them first, because each store decides what it can take that week. On a downsizing cleanout we take donations with us, so you don't have to wait for a pick-up date to clear the garage before possession.

Where is the best place to donate unwanted items from a garage?

It depends on the item. Working tools, hardware and leftover building materials suit a Habitat for Humanity ReStore, which has Calgary stores at 3465 Sunridge Way NE and 7291 11 Street SE. Usable bikes, sports gear and camping equipment can go to family, a neighbour or a charity that takes them. Anything with fuel in it needs to be drained or emptied first. Paint, propane and chemicals go to a City household hazardous waste drop-off, which is free.

How do I get rid of garage clutter before moving?

Work backwards from the possession date. Decide what is moving and mark it off, then let family take what they want, then donate, then send the hazardous waste to a City drop-off and take the rest to the landfill. Calgary landfills charge a flat $25 for a residential load under 250 kg in 2026, take tires and electronics free when they are kept separate, and add a $17 refrigerant fee for each fridge or freezer. Keep those streams apart, because a mixed load is charged as garbage.
