Our West Springs Garage Transformation Process

1

Measure & Design

We measure the garage precisely — walls, ceiling height, door swings, windows, the man-door, and the furnace and hot-water tank that eat into a corner. In a triple-car build that means mapping all three bays so the storage lands where it works and the vehicles still fit, all before a cabinet is built.

2

Declutter & Sort

Before installation we clear the backlog — the outgrown bikes, the boxes that never made it into the house, the gear from three seasons ago. Keep, donate, sell, dispose, handled with calm, judgment-free guidance, and usable items routed to donation rather than the landfill.

3

Floor First

If your project includes a floor coating, it goes in while the garage is empty. On a newer West Springs slab the prep is usually straightforward — a proper grind, crack repair where needed, then a polyaspartic or epoxy system chosen to shrug off road salt, hot tires, and chinook freeze-thaw.

4

Install Cabinets, Slatwall, Gear Walls & Overhead

Full-height cabinet towers for closed storage, slatwall and gear walls for the ski, bike, and sports kit, and overhead racks above the doors for seasonal bins — all lifted off the slab and planned so every vehicle still parks and every door still opens.

5

Walkthrough & Handover

We label, walk you through the layout, and leave a garage that stays organized because everything has an obvious place — including a simple plan for swapping summer and winter gear as the seasons turn on the west side.

The West Springs Garage Challenge — and How We Solve It

The Newer-Build Advantage — and Its Blind Spot

West Springs and Wentworth largely went up between the early 2000s and the mid-2010s, which is good news for a garage project: the slabs are relatively young, the walls are usually drywalled, and the ceilings are tall enough to go vertical. The blind spot is that builders hand over a big, blank double or triple garage with no storage at all, and families fill it organically over a decade — bikes here, bins there, a workbench that grew. The bones are excellent; what is missing is a plan. That is exactly the gap we close, and on a sound newer garage the work usually moves fast.

The Third-Bay Trap

Almost every triple-car garage in these communities starts the same way: three bays, three empty stalls, and every intention of parking three vehicles. Within a few years the third bay has quietly become the household's overflow room — seasonal decor, sports equipment, the deep-freeze, the stuff with no home inside. Then it spreads sideways into the second stall. We reverse that by building storage up the walls and overhead so the floor is freed, the overflow gets a real place, and the bay you were using for boxes goes back to being a bay for a vehicle.

A Backyard Full of Mountains

Few Calgary communities are as gear-heavy as West Springs, and geography is why. WinSport and Canada Olympic Park sit right at the community's edge — season-pass skiing, snowboarding, mountain biking, and the bobsled track minutes from the driveway — and the Trans-Canada shoots straight west to Kananaskis, Canmore, and Banff for the weekend. That adds up to real volume: alpine and touring skis, snowboards, several bikes, hydration packs, helmets, and boots for the whole family. We build dedicated gear walls and vertical racks so all of it is visible and reachable, and you can load the vehicle and go without excavating a pile in the corner.

West-Ridge Weather and Road Salt

Sitting high on the city's western edge, West Springs garages take the chinooks head-on — that dramatic winter swing from deep cold to above freezing and back that is so hard on concrete — plus a daily load of slush and road salt tracked in from the Bow Trail, Sarcee, and Trans-Canada commutes downtown or to the office parks in the west. Left bare, a slab pits, dusts, and picks up white salt staining every spring. A ground-and-coated polyaspartic or epoxy floor seals the concrete, brightens the space, and turns the annual salt cleanup into a quick squeegee.

Where the Mudroom Meets the Garage

Homes in West Springs and Wentworth are built for busy families, and most have a mudroom or boot room right off the garage. The problem is that the mudroom fills up because the garage side has nowhere to put the overflow — so bags, helmets, and boots stack on the family-entry bench. We plan the two spaces as one: a landing zone just inside the garage door with hooks, a cabinet run, and cubbies, so the sports kit and seasonal gear live on the garage side and the mudroom stays walkable.

Resale in a Fast Westside Market

West Springs and Wentworth are among Calgary's quickest-moving executive markets, and buyers here expect finished, considered spaces throughout the home. A blank builder garage stacked with totes reads as unfinished; a clean coated floor and built-in cabinetry photograph as a genuine feature and signal that the whole property has been well kept. In a competitive west-side listing, a garage that shows like the rest of the house is a quiet but real edge.

Do you organize the three-car garages common in West Springs and Wentworth?

We ski and bike at Canada Olympic Park — can you build storage for our gear?

Yes, and it is one of our favourite West Springs projects. With WinSport / Canada Olympic Park and the Paskapoo Slopes essentially in the backyard, and the Trans-Canada right there for weekend trips to Kananaskis and Canmore, a lot of local families move a genuine amount of gear. Is an epoxy or polyaspartic floor worth it on a west-Calgary garage?

For a daily-driven West Springs garage, yes.
