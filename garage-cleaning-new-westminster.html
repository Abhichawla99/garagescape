Why New Westminster Garages Are Their Own Job

New Westminster packs three genuinely different garage situations into six square kilometres, and knowing which one you're standing in changes the method.

The heritage problem

Queen's Park, Glenbrooke and Sapperton hold some of the oldest residential garages in the province — many built for cars a third the size of yours, with slabs poured when concrete practice was a different craft. That concrete is soft, porous, often already spalling, and it does not tolerate a full-pressure wash. We test every old slab in an inconspicuous corner and default to low-pressure chemical cleaning with wet extraction on anything fragile. Original board sheathing and unlined studs get the same respect: storage fixes into sound framing only, checked piece by piece, because hundred-year-old timber varies stud to stud.

The hillside problem

Most of the city climbs a steep slope from the Fraser, and Brow of the Hill earns its name. Garages cut into that grade carry the classic uphill-wall condition: saturated ground behind one wall, drainage from another era, water entering at the wall-slab junction, and mould establishing in the darkest corner before anyone notices. We find it, treat it, and write up what the drainage would actually need. Lane access on grades also means trucks sometimes can't get close — we plan the carry, not just the clean.

The delta problem

Queensborough is on Lulu Island — the same low-lying delta soil as Richmond, with the same shallow water table. Slab moisture and efflorescence are near-baseline there, floor-stored cardboard fails fast, and any floor coating plan should start with a moisture test. Everything on our Richmond page about delta garages applies to Queensborough word for word.

Old garages hold old materials

A note we make on every pre-1990 New Westminster job: vinyl floor tile, sheet-flooring backing, pipe wrap and some cement boards from that era can contain asbestos. If we suspect it, we stop and tell you, and testing comes before anything else moves. Given the age of this city's stock, it comes up more here than anywhere else we work — treat it as routine due diligence, not alarm.

New Westminster Garage Services — FAQs

How much does garage cleaning cost in New Westminster?

A single-car garage clean starts at $249 and a standard double at $349. Full declutter and organise runs $749–$1,100; cleanouts start at $399 plus disposal at cost. The Coquitlam Transfer Station is minutes away, which keeps disposal drive time off your bill.

Can you work on very old garages without damaging them?

Yes — it's most of what New West asks of us. Pre-war slabs are soft and porous and don't tolerate full pressure, so we test every one and default to low-pressure chemical cleaning on anything fragile. Storage fixes into sound framing only, checked stud by stud, because century-old timber varies.

Is Queensborough different from the rest of the city?

Meaningfully. It sits on Lulu Island delta soil — same shallow water table as Richmond — so slab moisture and efflorescence are near-baseline there. Up the hill, the bigger issues are uphill-wall drainage and lane access on grades.

Do you handle estate cleanouts here?

Frequently — long-held family homes in Queen's Park, Glenbrooke and Sapperton produce exactly this work. Nothing leaves without being seen, documents and photographs come back to you, and possible valuables get flagged for appraisal. In garages this old, interesting things surface more often than you'd think.

Which neighbourhoods do you cover?

All of them — Queen's Park, Sapperton, Glenbrooke, Brow of the Hill, the West End, Connaught Heights, Victory Heights, Massey Heights, downtown, the Quay and Queensborough, plus neighbouring Burnaby, Coquitlam and Surrey.

What if you find asbestos-era materials?

We stop, tell you, and testing comes before anything else moves. Pre-1990 vinyl flooring, pipe wrap and some cement boards can contain asbestos, and WorkSafeBC rules on handling it are strict. Given the age of New West's stock this is routine due diligence, not alarm — but it's non-negotiable.
