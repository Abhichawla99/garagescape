Our Mahogany Garage Transformation Process

1

Measure & Design

We measure your Mahogany garage precisely — depth, ceiling height, door swing, windows, the man-door, and the tandem or third bay that many estate homes here include. New garages give us room to work vertically, so we map the real space and design cabinetry, gear walls, and overhead storage that use every foot of that tall new ceiling.

2

Declutter & Sort

Even a newer home builds a backlog fast — moving boxes that never made it inside, doubled-up lake toys, packaging, and seasonal gear with no fixed home. We sort it with you: keep, donate, sell, dispose, handled with calm, judgment-free guidance, with usable items routed to donation rather than the landfill.

3

Floor First

If your project includes a floor coating, it goes in while the garage is empty. On a clean new Mahogany slab that means light prep and a proper diamond grind for adhesion, then a polyaspartic or epoxy system that seals the surface against road salt, gravel, hot tires, dripping lake gear, and chinook freeze-thaw — locked in before the concrete ever has a chance to stain.

4

Install Cabinets, Gear Walls & Overhead

Full-height cabinet towers for closed storage, slatwall and dedicated gear walls for the lake, skating, and bike kit, and overhead racks for seasonal bins and the off-season fleet — all lifted off the slab and planned so every bay still parks a vehicle and every door still opens, even with a full family fleet.

5

Walkthrough & Handover

We label, walk you through the layout, and leave a garage that stays organized because everything has an obvious place — including a simple plan for swapping the summer lake gear and the winter skates and shovels as the seasons turn on the lake.

The Mahogany Garage Story — and How We Solve It

Calgary's Largest Lake, and a New Kind of Garage

Mahogany is southeast Calgary's flagship lake community, master-planned around the city's largest freshwater lake with two private beaches, a 22,000-square-foot beach club, and a protected wetland threaded with pathways. It is one of the most decorated new communities in the region, and the homes reflect it: modern builds from the 2010s and 2020s with double and triple attached garages, tall ceilings, and clean slabs. That is a very different starting point from an older Calgary neighbourhood. Here the garage is not too small — it is a big, blank, brand-new space that fills with a family's fleet faster than anyone plans for, which is exactly why designing the storage early pays off so well.

How Mahogany Differs From an Older Lake Community

Calgary's original lake neighbourhoods were built in the 1960s and 70s around compact single and double bays, where the challenge is squeezing a modern fleet into a 1975 footprint. Mahogany is the opposite problem, and the more enjoyable one to solve. With tall new ceilings and a third bay in many homes, we have the vertical room to build a true system — floor-to-ceiling cabinet towers, a full lake-gear wall, dedicated overhead racks — rather than fighting for every inch. The risk in a new estate garage is not lack of space; it is that all that space quietly becomes an unplanned dumping ground. We get in early and turn it into a designed room instead.

A Serious Four-Season Lake Fleet

The lake is the entire point of living in Mahogany, and it drives the garage. In summer, private beach access means paddleboards, kayaks, canoes, life jackets, beach wagons, chairs, umbrellas, and towels for the whole family. When the lake freezes, the community clears a skating loop and the same garage has to hold skates, sticks, sleds, and toboggans. Add e-bikes and the pathway fleet for the wetlands trails, and a Mahogany garage is carrying more active gear than almost any other kind of home. We plan it as a four-season space: vertical racks and hoists for the big boards and boats, a hooks-and-bins station for skates and helmets by the door, a drip-and-dry zone for wet gear, and a simple summer-to-winter swap so what you are using is always in reach and the off-season kit is up on the wall.

Young, Active Families With Growing Fleets

Mahogany draws a lot of young families and move-up buyers, and family gear multiplies quickly: strollers turn into balance bikes, balance bikes into full bikes, and every kid adds a new sport. A triple garage feels endless the week you move in and cramped two summers later. We design with that growth in mind — adjustable slatwall and shelving that reconfigure as the kids get bigger, bike lifts that stack the family fleet vertically, and a labelled zone system that keeps the garage working even as what it holds keeps changing.

Protecting a New Slab From the First Chinook Winter

A brand-new Mahogany slab is clean, flat, and uncracked — the ideal moment to protect it. Calgary winters bring chinook freeze-thaw, that sharp swing from deep cold to above freezing and back that is punishing on concrete, plus road salt, grit, and dripping wet gear tracked in from the lake and the driveway. Coating early with a polyaspartic or epoxy system seals the surface before any of that reaches the concrete, so instead of watching a new floor slowly pit and stain, you get a bright, sealed bay that wipes clean for years. It is far cheaper and cleaner to protect a new slab than to grind and repair a damaged one later.

Westman Village and Downsizing in Style

Not every Mahogany resident wants a triple garage. Westman Village — the community's luxury lakeside enclave of villas and condominium residences with resort-style amenities — attracts downsizers and lock-and-leave owners who have traded square footage for a smaller, higher-finish space, often with heated, private, or underground parking. They still keep premium bikes, e-bikes, golf clubs, and lake gear, and they still want it beautifully organized. For these residences we design compact, high-finish cabinetry and gear walls that make a smaller footprint feel effortless and match the standard of the home.

Resale and Finish in a Premium SE Community

Mahogany is one of southeast Calgary's most sought-after addresses, and buyers here are finish-conscious. When a garage is part of the walkthrough, an unfinished bay stacked with totes reads as a to-do list, while built-in cabinetry and a clean coated floor photograph as a genuine feature and signal a home cared for right through to the garage. In a community where the lake, the amenities, and the newness of the homes set a high bar, a finished garage quietly meets that bar rather than undercutting it.
