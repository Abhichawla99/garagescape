How Many Truckloads Is Your Garage?

Junk companies price by volume, so measure volume. Take a tape measure to each pile and write down three numbers in feet: how long it runs along the wall, how deep it comes off the wall, and how high it's stacked. Multiply them. Add the piles together, then divide by the size of the truck. The company that published its full load as 12 by 8 by 5 feet is describing 480 cubic feet, so that's the truck we use here.

Three Calgary garages, worked through:

  • Shelving along one side of a single garage. 18 feet long, 2 feet deep, 6 feet high is 216 cubic feet, a little under half a truck. Add the shelving units themselves if they're going too.
  • The back wall of a double garage. A pile 20 feet wide, 4 feet deep and 4 feet high is 320 cubic feet, two thirds of a truck, before anyone looks in the rafters.
  • A single bay you can't walk into. 11 feet wide, 20 feet deep and stacked 5 feet high is 1,100 cubic feet, more than two full trucks. This is the garage in the photo below, and it's the one where a "full truck" quote turns into two.

Round up. A box stacks tight on a shelf, but a bike, a lawn chair and a hockey bag never pack as neatly in a truck as they sit against a wall. Rafters and the space above the door count too.

Even a full tonne of garbage is $113 at the City scale. Unless the load is very heavy, most of a $500 to $675 truckload price is the truck, the crew's time and the number of separate trips. When a quote seems high, ask how many stops it covers: landfill, hazardous waste drop-off, donation, and sometimes a scrap metal yard are four different places.

The City also lists a $275 surcharge for loads it classes as hard to handle, and it requires every load to be covered and secured. Both matter if you're hauling it yourself.

The Do-It-Yourself Total

For some garages the cheapest answer is a few trips in your own vehicle. Add it up honestly first:

  1. Sort into streams before you load. Hazardous waste in one pile, tires and electronics in another, donations in a third, garbage last. A mixed load costs you the free streams.
  2. Count your trips. Each landfill visit under 250 kg is $25. Five trips in a minivan is $125 plus five drives to the landfill and back.
  3. Add the drop-offs. The hazardous waste drop-off is a fire hall, and it isn't the landfill. Lincoln Park/Garrison Green Fire Station #20 at 2800 Peacekeepers Way SW takes household hazardous waste from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. every day.
  4. Price the heavy pieces. A chest freezer, a gun safe or a weight stack needs two people and a vehicle that can carry it. If you're renting a truck for one item, add that.
  5. Check the weather. The City doesn't plow back lanes. A garage on a lane in Lakeview, Altadore or Mount Royal can be hard to load from between November and March.

Put that total next to the quotes. If it fits in a weekend and comes in under the cheapest quote, do it yourself. When it's more than your vehicle can carry in a weekend, or the freezer is the problem, compare a bin against a crew or get a fixed price for the whole garage.

Seven Questions That Make Two Quotes Comparable

Ask every company the same seven questions, including us, and write down the answers:

  1. Is the landfill fee in the price? Some prices include disposal. The hourly and bin prices we read on 14 November listed dump fees separately.
  2. Is there a charge per fridge, freezer, tire or mattress? The City charges $17 per refrigerant appliance. Companies set their own per-item fees on top, or none.
  3. Will you take the paint, propane and batteries? If not, that's your trip to the fire hall.
  4. Is the price fixed before you load, or set by what fits on the truck? A photo quote and a truck-fraction price can both be fair, but they're different promises.
  5. What gets donated, and where? Bikes, tools and camping gear in working order don't have to go over the scale.
  6. Are you insured? We carry $2 million in general liability insurance and can send the certificate to a condo board or property manager.
  7. Does the price include sweeping the floor? If you're selling the house or handing it to a tenant, an empty garage that hasn't been swept still needs another visit.

Our answer to all seven is in one quote. We sort before loading, take hazardous waste, tires, electronics and appliances to the right place, take usable things to donation, include the landfill and refrigerant fees, and sweep before we leave.

How much does the dump cost in Calgary?

In 2026 a City of Calgary landfill charges a flat $25 for a residential garbage load of 250 kg or less. Over 250 kg, the whole load is charged by weight at $113 a tonne for garbage. Fridges, freezers and air conditioners carry a $17 refrigerant fee each plus weight. Tires and electronics are free when kept separate, but if free items are mixed into a charged load, the whole load is charged. Loads must be covered and secured.

What is the cheapest way to get rid of junk in Calgary?

Sort it and haul it yourself. Paint, propane cylinders under 30 lb, car batteries and motor oil are free at a City household hazardous waste drop-off, up to 20 L per visit. Tires and electronics are free at the landfill when kept apart from garbage. Usable things can be donated or sold. What's left costs $25 a load at a City landfill if it's under 250 kg. It stops being cheapest when the garage needs more trips than you have weekends, or when the heavy pieces need two people and a truck.

Is there free junk removal in Calgary?

Not for a whole garage. The City's Community Cleanups, where residents bring large household items to a City garbage truck, ran from April 25 to September 27 in 2026, and the City says to check back in spring 2027 for the next schedule. Household hazardous waste drop-offs are free, tires and electronics are free at the landfill when separate, and Habitat for Humanity Southern Alberta ReStores offer free pick-up for qualifying donations such as tools and hardware.

Where do I dispose of yard waste in Calgary?

Leaves, grass and garden waste go in the green cart. From the week of November 2, 2026 the green cart is collected every other week, and weekly pick-up resumes in April 2027. Bags of old soil and potting mix from the garage can go back into a garden bed instead of to the landfill.
