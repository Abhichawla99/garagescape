Burnaby's Two Garage Types Need Different Storage

Most of Burnaby's housing stock splits into two eras, and each one puts a different constraint on storage. The first is post-war detached housing in Burnaby Heights, Capitol Hill, Willingdon Heights and older pockets of Deer Lake, mostly built between the 1940s and 1960s with attached or detached single and double garages. The second, and it's the one that defines Burnaby now, is townhouse. Density growth around Brentwood, Metrotown, Edmonds and Lougheed brought a very large volume of tandem garages: one bay wide, two cars long, a single door at one end, with the furnace, hot water tank and often the electrical panel built into the room.

A tandem garage fails at storage in a specific way. Shelving down both walls looks efficient on paper and makes the space unusable in practice, because the aisle disappears and a second car no longer fits, which was the entire point of having a two-car-long garage. The back third also becomes dead storage once anything blocks the path to it, since reaching it means moving everything stacked in front. A detached garage in Burnaby Heights has the opposite problem: enough width for shelving on both sides, but a slab and wall cavities that are five or six decades old, with moisture behaviour that a modern garage doesn't have.

Burnaby Mountain and the elevation effect

Properties toward SFU, Forest Grove and the Burnaby Mountain slopes sit well above the valley floor and get measurably more rain, and heavy conifer canopy holds moisture against exterior walls year-round. Storage installed flush against an outside wall up there is more exposed than the same install in south Burnaby, so the air-gap and raised-riser rules matter more the higher up the mountain a property sits.

Strata Rules: What You're Actually Allowed to Store

This is the part almost nobody selling garage storage in Burnaby addresses, and it decides the project before a single shelf goes up. Under BC's strata legislation, the garage or parking stall attached to a townhouse unit is either common property, owned proportionally by every owner in the building, or limited common property, which gives one owner exclusive use rights without full ownership. Strata bylaws can restrict what's kept in either one, and a bylaw commonly bans storing furniture or combustible materials in a parking or garage area on top of the general BC Fire Code restriction on combustible storage in parking structures.

Two details catch Burnaby owners out most often. Exclusive-use rights over a garage or stall attach to the owner, not the strata lot, so they don't automatically transfer when you buy resale, and the strata council can reassign them at its discretion. And your Form B Information Certificate, along with the current bylaws, is where the real rules for your specific building live, not a general assumption about what's normal. Some Burnaby stratas require written approval before anything is fixed to a shared garage wall. Check both before you spend money on a system that has to be fixed rather than freestanding.

A five-minute check that saves a rebuild

We've had Burnaby clients spec a full wall-mounted shelving system, then find out at install that the strata required sign-off first. Pull your Form B and bylaws, or ask your strata manager directly, before ordering anything permanent. If your strata is strict about fixed installs, freestanding steel units on risers get you almost the same capacity without touching a wall.

Overhead Racking and Ceiling Height

Overhead racking is the highest-value addition for either Burnaby garage type, because it puts bulky seasonal items in the one part of the room that's driest, since warm air rises away from an unheated slab. Two checks matter before buying anything. First, measure to the lowest obstruction, not the highest point, and check the garage door track's full swing clearance. Second, have the ceiling joists assessed before hanging a rack rated at 250 to 600 lb. Detached garages in Burnaby Heights, Capitol Hill and Willingdon Heights that date from the 1940s to 1960s can have undersized framing by current standards, or joists weakened where a roof has leaked, and a tandem townhouse garage's ceiling often carries ductwork and plumbing that limits where a rack can go at all.

Bins, Wall Systems and the Mistakes We Fix Most Often

Bin choice matters more in Burnaby than the material of the shelf underneath it. Use gasketed opaque bins, not snap-lid clear ones. The gasket is what keeps humidity out, and opaque walls protect contents from light and let you rely on labelling instead of visibility. Never use cardboard. Keep bins off the slab on risers, and leave a small air gap behind anything mounted on an exterior wall, since flush mounting is what creates the condensation trap behind a shelf that nobody notices until the mould shows up two years later.

On wall systems specifically: PVC slatwall is the most flexible choice for a detached garage with a changing storage load, metal or PVC pegboard is the cheapest useful option over a workbench, and heavy direct-mount hooks into framing are the least flexible but cheapest choice for bikes, ladders and hoses.

  1. Shelving on both walls of a tandem garage. The single most common request we talk clients out of, because it closes the aisle and the second car stops fitting.
  2. MDF cabinets against an exterior wall. Swollen within two winters, mould behind, in either garage type.
  3. Fixed installs without checking the strata bylaws first. Some Burnaby stratas require sign-off, and finding out after install means removing it.
  4. Overhead racks bought before measuring ceiling height. Common in older Burnaby Heights garages with lower ceilings and ductwork through tandem garage ceilings.
  5. Cardboard box storage. Collapses, wicks moisture, and is the first thing rodents move into.

Burnaby Garage Storage FAQs

What's the best garage storage for a Burnaby tandem townhouse garage?

Narrow-depth steel shelving down one wall only, never both. A tandem garage is one bay wide and two cars long, so shelving on both walls closes the aisle and you lose the ability to park a second car, which defeats the point of the garage. We run adjustable steel rail down the wall opposite the furnace and hot water tank, keep the full walking channel clear, and put anything rarely used at the back on the highest shelf rather than the floor.

Can my strata restrict what I store in my Burnaby garage or parking stall?

Yes. Under BC's strata law, a garage or parking stall is either common property or limited common property, and the strata corporation can adopt bylaws restricting what is kept there, including furniture and combustible material. Your Form B Information Certificate and the strata bylaws set the actual rules for your building, and exclusive-use rights attach to the owner rather than the unit, so they don't automatically carry over if you bought resale. Check both before buying a fixed shelving system, and confirm with the strata council before drilling into a shared wall.

How much does garage storage cost in Burnaby?

A one-wall system for a tandem townhouse garage typically runs $250 to $600 in materials, since only one side gets shelving. A full double garage system in a Burnaby Heights or Capitol Hill detached home runs $600 to $2,500 depending on overhead racking and gear storage. Add our design and installation service from $549. Custom cabinetry from a Metro Vancouver manufacturer starts around $4,000 for a double garage.

Are overhead storage racks safe in an older Burnaby Heights garage?

Only after the joists are checked. Many detached garages in Burnaby Heights, Capitol Hill and Willingdon Heights date from the 1940s to 1960s, and ceiling framing from that era can be undersized by current standards or weakened where a roof has leaked. We assess the structure before hanging anything rated at 250 to 600 lb, and on older garages that sometimes means adding blocking or recommending wall storage instead.

Why does Burnaby Mountain cause more storage problems than the rest of Burnaby?

Elevation and tree cover. Properties toward SFU, Forest Grove and the mountain slopes sit well above the valley floor and get noticeably more rain, and heavy conifer canopy holds that moisture against structures. Anything stored against an exterior wall up there is at higher risk of the mould and moss problems we see less often in south Burnaby, so the air-gap and raised-storage rules matter more, not less, the higher up the mountain you are.

Do you install garage storage everywhere in Burnaby?

Yes, across all of Burnaby: Brentwood, Metrotown, Edmonds, Lougheed, Burnaby Heights, Capitol Hill, Willingdon Heights, Deer Lake, Burnaby Mountain, Big Bend and everywhere between, plus neighbouring New Westminster and Coquitlam. If you're near a boundary, just ask when you book.

What's the cheapest way to get useful storage into a Burnaby garage?

One wall of adjustable steel shelving, gasketed opaque bins instead of cardboard, and a set of heavy wall hooks for bikes and long-handled tools. In a tandem garage that's usually $250 to $450 in materials and delivers most of the practical benefit, because the second wall in a tandem space needs to stay clear for the car anyway.
