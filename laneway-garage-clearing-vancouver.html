Where This Fits in Your Laneway Project

Vancouver's laneway housing program puts a detached home of up to roughly 900 square feet on the back of an RS-zoned lot — almost always on the footprint the garage currently occupies. Between you and that new home sits a structure holding, in our experience, somewhere between five and fifty years of accumulation.

What the Job Includes

  • Full contents removal and five-way sort — keep, sell, donate, stewardship, dispose. Nothing leaves unseen; this is often a garage the family has owned for decades and things turn up.
  • Free-channel routing — old lane garages are dense with exactly what disposes free in BC: paint, oil, batteries, tools worth donating, scrap metal with value, returnables. Full detail on our cleanout page.
  • Hazard identification in writing — suspected asbestos-era materials, buried fuel cans, chemical stashes. Your demolition contractor needs a hazmat assessment for permits anyway; our flag list gives it a head start.
  • No-water, no-power capability — most lane garages have neither. We bring both.
  • Documented handover — the empty structure photographed, so what was left inside (nothing) is never a dispute between you and the builder.

What we're not

We don't demolish, disconnect utilities, or pull permits — that's your builder's or a licensed demolition contractor's scope. And if you're keeping the garage and building elsewhere on the lot, what you likely want instead is our standard garage cleanout or deep clean. This page is specifically the pre-demolition version: everything out, structure handed over empty.

Laneway Garage Clearing — FAQs

Why clear the garage before the contractor mobilises?

Money and care. Once a crew is on site, garage contents get handled at trade rates or skipped wholesale — including things you'd have kept, donated or sold. Cleared beforehand, it's a considered sort at cleanout prices, and demolition day starts on schedule.

How much does it cost?

Most single lane garages clear for $449–$800 all in, depending on volume and lane access. Heavy accumulation or hazardous materials push it higher; we quote on site. Against construction labour doing the same lifting at day rates, the arithmetic settles itself.

What if there's asbestos-era material in there?

We stop and flag it in writing before anything else moves. Your demolition contractor needs a hazmat assessment for permits anyway — finding out during the contents clear, months early, beats finding out the week the excavator arrives.

Do you demolish the garage too?

No — that's your builder's or a licensed demolition contractor's scope, with its own permits and utility disconnects. We do the step before: everything out, sorted and routed, hazards flagged, structure photographed and handed over empty.

When should this happen?

During design and permitting — months before mobilisation — when decisions can be made properly. Leaving it to the week before demolition turns a considered sort into a panic skip-fill. If you're in permitting now, now is the time.

Where do the contents go?

Reuse first, stewardship second, recycling third, landfill last — itemised at cost with receipts. Old lane garages are full of exactly the categories that dispose free, which keeps these jobs cheaper than people expect.
