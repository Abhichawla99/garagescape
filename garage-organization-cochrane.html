Garage Organization in Cochrane, Alberta

Cochrane roughly tripled in population between 2001 and 2021, from about 11,000 people to about 32,000, and most of those households moved into garages built in the last 25 years. By November those garages are holding the trailer gear that had to come off the driveway, the paddleboards from the Bow, the second set of tires, while the car sits in the driveway. What a Cochrane Garage Has to Hold

Trailer Gear in the Off-Season

Town peace officers have told residents that trailers can sit on a driveway from May 1 to October 22. After that, the trailer goes to storage and its contents come into the garage. We give that kit a dedicated shelf zone so it does not end up under the car.

River and Lake Kit

The Bow runs through the middle of town, Ghost Lake is a short drive west on Highway 1A, and Mitford Park has fishing access. Rods, paddles, boards and PFDs are long, awkward and wet in the fall. They go on the wall or the ceiling.

Wind and Chinook Weather

Cochrane sits in the foothills where strong westerlies come off the mountains. In December 2021 forecasters called for gusts of 80 to 90 km/h through the area. Bins, ladders and patio furniture that live in the driveway in summer need a winter spot inside.

Winter on the Big Hill

Most commuters drive Highway 1A or 22 into Calgary through the winter. That means winter tires on, summer tires stored, a snowblower parked near the door, and salt and gravel tracking onto the slab every day from November to April.

The November Garage, Before and After

Why the pile shows up now

The driveway season ends in late October. Camping chairs, coolers, the propane stove, sewer hoses, leveling blocks and a summer of bikes all move indoors in the same week, usually stacked in front of whatever was already there. Two weeks later the first snow arrives and the car is parked outside.

What we change

We pull everything out, sort it into keep, donate and dispose, then put back only what has a defined home. Trailer gear goes into labelled bins on one shelving run so it can load back in one trip. Daily items sit between knee and shoulder height near the house door. Seasonal items go up.

Planning by Garage Type

Late-1990s doubles in West Terrace and Glenbow

West Terrace homes are mostly 1,100 to 1,800 sq ft and the attached garages are sized to match: a true double with little spare width. Floor-standing shelving eats the space a car door needs, so we go vertical. One shelving wall at the back, wall hooks on the side walls above door height, and an overhead rack over the hood for anything used twice a year.

Walkout homes in Heritage Hills and GlenEagles

Hillside lots give many of these homes a walkout basement, and the temptation is to make the basement the overflow room. That works for paper, decorations and keepsakes. It does not work for anything that comes home wet, salty or smelling of fuel. We plan the garage to take the cold, dirty and heavy items so the walkout stays a room people use.

Laned homes in Riversong and Heritage Hills

Both communities offer laned options alongside front-drive homes. A rear garage is often narrower, sometimes single-width, and it is the only covered storage on the lot. Here every wall counts: slatwall for bikes and yard tools, a shallow cabinet run that clears the mirror, and ceiling storage for the seasonal bins.

Newer builds in Fireside, Sunset Ridge and Heartland

A builder-finished garage is a blank box with one bulb and an outlet. The best time to plan it is before the moving boxes find a corner. We measure the footprint, map where each vehicle and the snowblower go, and install shelving and hooks in the first visit so the garage starts with a system.

A Storage Plan for River, Trail and Camping Gear

Long items go up or on the wall

Fly rods, paddles, a paddleboard, cross-country skis and a pole saw all share one problem: they are too long for a shelf and they fall over in a corner. A wall rack mounted above car-door height or a ceiling hoist keeps them straight, dry and out of the walkway.

Bikes on hooks, not on kickstands

Riversong alone has 19 km of pathways, and the river trail system links most of town. A family of four can easily own six bikes. Vertical hooks on a side wall store them in about a metre of width, with helmets and pumps in a bin directly below.

Small kit in labelled bins

Tackle, PFDs, headlamps, bear spray, camp kitchen and hockey gear all go into clear bins with labels you can read from the door. When the trailer comes back onto the driveway in May, the bins it needs are on one shelf.

Sorting for the Cochrane Eco Centre

Paint and chemicals

Every garage we organize has half-used paint cans and a shelf of chemicals. The Town of Cochrane Eco Centre accepts paint cans and aerosols up to 30 L per household per week, and other household hazardous waste up to 10 L per household per week, labelled and sealed. We group these by type during the sort so the drop-off is quick and stays within the limits.

Electronics, metal and appliances

The Eco Centre takes electronics (anything with a cord or battery) and has a mixed-metal bin for scrap. Old fridges and freezers carry a fee there, as do stoves, washers, dryers and hot water tanks. Knowing that up front helps you decide whether the garage freezer stays.

Tires do not go to the Eco Centre

The Eco Centre does not accept tires and directs residents to return them to a retailer or dealership. Old tires get set aside during the sort. The tires you keep go on a wall-mounted rack or a dedicated shelf off the floor.

Garage Organization in Cochrane: Common Questions

Do you organize garages in Cochrane, or only in Calgary?

Cochrane is part of our regular service area. We already run garage decluttering jobs in Cochrane, and organization projects are booked the same way, with a free quote first, usually from a few photos you text us.

My trailer had to come off the driveway for winter. Where does the gear inside it go?

The Town of Cochrane has told residents that trailers and RVs may sit on driveways from May 1 to October 22, so by November the camping gear, coolers, chairs and hoses that lived in the trailer often land on the garage floor. We give that gear its own labelled bins on shelving or an overhead rack, so it stays dry all winter and loads back into the trailer in one trip in May.

We have a walkout basement. Should storage go there or in the garage?

Split it by what the item can tolerate. Anything that freezes, rusts or carries road salt and gas smell (tires, a snowblower, fuel cans, sports gear that comes in wet) belongs in the garage. Paper, photos, holiday decorations and anything you only touch once a year usually do better downstairs. We plan the garage around that split so the walkout stays living space.

What do you do with the paint, chemicals and old tires we clear out?

We sort them before anything is hauled. The Cochrane Eco Centre takes paint and aerosols up to 30 L per household per week and other household hazardous waste up to 10 L per household per week, labelled and sealed. It does not take tires, which go back to a tire retailer or dealer. We set those items aside so nothing ends up in the wrong bin.

Can you store fishing rods, paddleboards and bikes without them taking over the garage?

Yes. Long items like rods, paddles, skis and a paddleboard go on wall racks or ceiling hoists above the hood of the car, bikes hang on vertical hooks, and the small kit (tackle, PFDs, helmets, pumps) goes into labelled bins at grabbing height.

How long does a Cochrane garage organization project take?

Most single and double garages are finished in one visit. Larger jobs with cabinetry, overhead racks or a heavy cleanout first can need a second visit. We confirm the timeline in your free quote before any work starts.

Do you also do epoxy floors, shelving and cabinets in Cochrane?

Yes. Organization can be paired with custom shelving, cabinets and garage flooring, so the floor is done before the storage system goes back in.
