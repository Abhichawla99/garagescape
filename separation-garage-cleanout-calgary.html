Garage Cleanout During a Separation or Divorce in Calgary

One of you has moved out, the house may already be listed, and the garage still holds both of your things. We clear it from a list you've both agreed to, send the same photos to both of you, and leave anything still in dispute exactly where it is.

The garage is usually the last part of the house a separating couple divides. The kitchen gets sorted when someone moves out. The garage keeps the tool chest, the snowblower, four bikes, two sets of winter tires, the camping gear and the boxes nobody unpacked after the last move. When the house goes on the market, all of it has to be somewhere else by possession day, and often one person ends up doing it alone while the other wonders what happened to their things.

So we work to one rule. We take instructions from a list both of you have agreed to in writing, from a letter your lawyers have both signed off on, or from a court order. That protects the person who is still living in the house as much as the person who moved out. Nobody can say later that the crew threw out the good drill on one person's say-so.

In Alberta, the Family Property Act sets how property is divided when a relationship ends. It was called the Matrimonial Property Act until January 1, 2020, when it was renamed and extended to adult interdependent partners as well as married spouses. A court can also give one person possession of the family home while the rest is worked out. We aren't lawyers and nothing here is legal advice. It's the reason we ask for the paperwork before the truck shows up.

Seven Tags, and One Shelf Nothing Leaves

Every item in the garage gets one of seven tags before anything is loaded. We use coloured tape and write the tag on it, so either of you can walk in and read the garage at a glance. The tags come from your list. Where the list is silent, the item goes to "not decided".

The "not decided" shelf is what makes the rest of the job possible. Couples who agree on nine-tenths of the garage can clear that nine-tenths now, before the listing photos, and leave the argument about the table saw for later. Paint, fuel, propane and old motor oil never get a name tag. Nobody wants them, and they go to a City household hazardous waste drop-off at a fire station.

One Set of Photos, Sent to Both of You

Before anything moves, we photograph the garage from the door, each wall, the rafters and the overhead storage. Then we photograph individual items that could matter later: anything with a brand and model on it, anything with a serial number (tools, bikes, the snowblower, the generator), and every item on the "not decided" shelf.

  • Everyone on the instruction list gets the same photos at the same time. One shared folder or one group message. Nobody gets a different set.
  • You confirm the tags in writing before we load. A reply of "yes" from each of you in the group text is enough.
  • At the end, you both get the after photos and a list of what went where: each person's items, the donation drop-off, the landfill run with its weigh ticket, the hazardous waste drop-off.
  • Our crew stays out of the middle. They don't pass messages, they don't offer opinions on who should get what, and they don't discuss one person's things with the other beyond what's on the list.

If you have lawyers, you can tell us to send everything to them instead. That set of photos, the list and the receipts is a record either side can rely on if a question comes up during negotiations.

Two Addresses, and Nobody Has to Meet

Most garage cleanouts end at one place: the landfill. In a separation, one person's share usually needs to reach a second address, and the two of you may not want to be in the driveway together. We plan for that.

  1. Delivery to the second address. If one of you has moved to an apartment, a condo or a storage unit, we load their tagged items separately and drop them there. Condo buildings usually want the service elevator booked; give us the property manager's contact and we'll book it.
  2. Pickup windows. If the person who moved out wants to collect their own things, we can hold them in the garage for a pickup window they book with us, at a time the other person has agreed not to be home.
  3. Split days. We can run the job over two days, one for each of you to be present for your own share, so you never overlap.
  4. No one present at all. A lockbox or door code, the agreed list and our photos are enough to do the job.

If there's a protection order, or you're worried about running into each other, tell us when you book. We won't schedule you both for the same window, and we won't give one person's new address to the other. Kids' things often split by child rather than by parent. If the bikes, hockey bags and sleds should follow a particular child to a particular house, tag them by the child's name and we'll load them that way.

When the House Is Sold as Part of the Split

Many Calgary separations end with the house sold and the money divided. That puts two dates on the garage. For the listing photos, it needs to look like room for cars. By possession day, it needs to be empty: the standard Alberta purchase contract gives the buyer vacant possession, usually at noon on the completion day. Our garage cleanout before selling page works back from both dates.

In a separation sale, the realtor is often the first person to see the garage problem, because neither owner wants to raise it. We're happy to have the realtor as our contact on the day, to meet them for a lockbox and to send them the after photos. The instructions about what leaves still come from both owners.

Two Calgary details for this time of year. The City doesn't plow back lanes, so a lane garage in an inner-city neighbourhood like Mount Royal or Lakeview can be hard to reach after a heavy snowfall. Leave a few days of margin before possession. And Calgary has been under Stage 4 outdoor water restrictions since November 4, 2026, so the floor gets a dry sweep at the end, with no hose. If the buyer's walk-through is going to notice oil on the slab, our oil stain removal page explains what can be done without water.

The Quote, and Who Pays It

We quote from photos before booking. A separation cleanout takes more work per box than a straight haul-away, and these are the things that move the number:

  • Volume and weight. City of Calgary landfill fees are $25 for a residential load of 250 kg or less and $113 a tonne above that. Tires and electronics are free if kept out of the garbage load.
  • Fridges and freezers, at $17 each for the refrigerant at the landfill.
  • The photo record and tagging, which takes longer in a garage with a lot of tools and equipment.
  • A second address, and whether it's a house, a condo with a service elevator, or a storage unit.
  • Split days, if you want each person present for their own share.
  • The "not decided" shelf, if it has to move to a storage unit so the garage can be empty for possession.
  • The carry from a lane garage or a tight side gate to the truck.

On payment: we can address one invoice to one of you, or split it into two invoices in whatever proportion you've agreed, so each person pays their own part. If you want it paid out of the sale proceeds, sort that out with your lawyers; the invoice will show the property address and the split. Questions From Separating Couples

Who should pay for cleaning out the garage before the house is sold in a divorce?

That's for the two of you, or your lawyers, to agree. Some couples split it evenly, some put it on whoever is staying in the house until the sale, and some pay it from the sale proceeds. We can address one invoice to one of you, or split it into two invoices in whatever proportion you've agreed. Ask your lawyer before paying from proceeds.

How much does it cost to clear out a garage in Calgary?

It depends on volume, weight and how much sorting the job needs. One Calgary junk removal company's site said most cleanouts run between $300 and $1,000 when we read it on November 27, 2026. City of Calgary landfill fees are $25 for a residential load of 250 kg or less and $113 a tonne above that, plus $17 for each fridge or freezer. A separation cleanout adds photo records, sorting by person and sometimes a second drop-off address. We quote from photos before booking.

How long does it take to clean out a garage?

A garage that is only being hauled away goes faster than one being divided. Photographing everything first, sorting by person and loading two destinations all take time. We give you the expected length with the quote, and we can split the job over two days so each of you can be there for your own things without overlapping.

How long does my ex have to remove their things from the garage?

We can't give you a legal deadline, and we don't know of a garage-specific one. Agree a pickup date in writing, through your lawyers if you have them. If your ex agrees in writing that we can deliver their items or clear them by a date, we'll work to that date. We won't remove one person's belongings on the other person's instruction alone.

Do we both have to be there for the cleanout?

No. Neither of you has to be there, and you never have to be there at the same time. We work from the agreed list, send the same before photos to both of you, and send the after photos and a list of where everything went when we finish.

What happens to things neither of us has decided on?

They go on the 'not decided' shelf. We photograph each item, tape off a corner of the garage for them or move them to a storage unit one of you has rented, and nothing on that shelf leaves until both of you, or both lawyers, tell us where it goes.

Will you tell my ex where I'm moving?

No. If we deliver your things to a new address, that address stays with us. Our crew doesn't pass messages between the two of you, and if there is a protection order or you're worried about running into each other, tell us and we'll schedule so that can't happen.

Are you insured?

Yes. GarageScape carries $2 million in general liability insurance and can send proof before the job, to you, your realtor or your lawyer.

What won't you move during a separation cleanout?

We don't move vehicles, trailers, boats or firearms, and we don't sell anything for you. We don't read paperwork we find; financial records and personal documents are boxed, sealed and handled the way both of you instruct. Paint, fuel and propane go to a City household hazardous waste drop-off, never into the garbage load.
