Our Lake Bonavista Garage Transformation Process

1

Measure & Design

We measure the garage precisely — depth, ceiling height, the door swing, windows, the man-door, and the furnace or hot-water tank that so often eats into a corner of an original Lake Bonavista bay. In a tight older garage, every inch matters, so we map the real space before a single cabinet is built and design storage that fits it exactly.

2

Declutter & Sort

Before installation we clear the backlog that decades in a family home accumulate — outgrown lake toys, old paint, boxes that never made it into the house, and gear from three seasons ago. Keep, donate, sell, dispose, handled with calm, judgment-free guidance, with usable items routed to donation rather than the landfill.

3

Floor First

If your project includes a floor coating, it goes in while the garage is empty. On an aging Lake Bonavista slab that means a proper grind, crack and spall repair for concrete that has seen fifty-plus winters, then a polyaspartic or epoxy system chosen to seal the surface against road salt, gravel, hot tires, dripping lake gear, and chinook freeze-thaw.

4

Install Cabinets, Gear Walls & Overhead

Full-height cabinet towers for closed storage, slatwall and dedicated gear walls for the lake, skating, and bike kit, and overhead racks for seasonal bins and the off-season fleet — all lifted off the slab and planned so the vehicle still parks and every door still opens, even in a compact original garage.

5

Walkthrough & Handover

We label, walk you through the layout, and leave a garage that stays organized because everything has an obvious place — including a simple plan for swapping the summer lake gear and the winter skates and shovels as the seasons turn on the lake.

The Lake Bonavista Garage Challenge — and How We Solve It

Canada's First Lake Community, and Its First-Generation Garages

Lake Bonavista holds a genuine place in Calgary history: developed by Keith Homes beginning in the late 1960s, it was Canada's first man-made lake community and set the template every later lake neighbourhood copied. That heritage is a big part of its charm — mature trees, wide streets, a private 52-acre lake, and roughly 4,100 homes housing over 10,000 residents — but it also means the garages are first-generation. Original single and double bays from the 1970s and 80s were built for one or two cars and a lawnmower, not for a modern household's paddleboards, bikes, hockey bags, and lake fleet. The result is a garage doing a 2026 job in a 1975 footprint, and the fix is never more space; it is a plan that uses every wall and the ceiling.

Small-Garage Smarts for Original Bays

This is where Lake Bonavista differs from the newer estate communities west of the city. Instead of a triple or quad garage with room to spare, many homes here have a shallow, lower-ceilinged original double where the furnace or hot-water tank steals a corner and the only storage is a shelf someone nailed to the studs decades ago. We treat that as a design problem, not a lost cause: precise measuring, then full-height cabinet towers on the back wall, slatwall for the gear you touch weekly, and overhead racks tucked above the door for the off-season bins. Done well, a compact original garage swallows the clutter and still parks the car — which, through a Calgary winter, is the whole point.

A Four-Season Lake Fleet

The private lake is what makes this neighbourhood, and it makes the garage work in a way we do not see anywhere else. In summer the lake means paddleboards, kayaks, canoes, life jackets, beach chairs, towels, and fishing gear. In winter that same lake freezes for skating and shinny, the community centre runs two indoor rinks, and out come the skates, sticks, sleds, and toboggans. A single garage has to hold both fleets and let the family swap between them without a wrestling match. We plan it as a four-season space: vertical racks and hoists for the big boards and boats, a hooks-and-bins station for skates and helmets by the door, a drip-and-dry zone for wet gear, and a simple summer-to-winter swap so what you are using is always in reach and the off-season kit is up on the wall.

Backing Onto Fish Creek

Lake Bonavista's southern edge runs along Fish Creek Provincial Park, one of the largest urban parks in Canada, so this is a cycling, walking, and cross-country-ski neighbourhood on top of a lake one. Bikes for the whole family, jogging strollers, trailers, trail packs, and winter skis all need a home that keeps them ready and off the floor. We add wall hooks and bike lifts, a helmet-and-pack station near the door, and a clean staging spot so heading out to the park pathways is a two-minute affair rather than an archaeology dig in the corner.

Aging Slabs and Fifty Winters of Chinooks

An original Lake Bonavista slab has earned its wear. Fifty-plus Calgary winters of chinook freeze-thaw — that dramatic swing from deep cold to above freezing and back that is so punishing on concrete — plus road salt, grit, and years of dripping gear leave a floor that is pitted, dusty, and stained. For an older garage this is often the highest-impact upgrade we do: we grind the slab back, repair the cracks and spalled patches that come with age, then lay a polyaspartic or epoxy system that seals the concrete, brightens a dim older bay, and turns the yearly salt-and-dust mess into a quick squeegee. On a renovated home it also makes the garage look as current as the kitchen inside.

Renovations, Rebuilds, and Lake Bonavista Estates

Not every garage here is a compact original. Lake Bonavista has been renovating and rebuilding for years, and Lake Bonavista Estates — the community's premium sub-area of larger lots — carries bigger, higher-finish garages that deserve the full treatment. For these we scale up: showroom-grade custom cabinetry, dedicated gear walls, workshop zones, and a coated floor, designed to match the standard of a home that has been brought up to date. Whether we are rescuing a 1978 double or outfitting a rebuilt estate garage, the goal is the same — a garage that finally matches the way the family actually lives on the lake.

Resale in a Fast SE Market

Lake Bonavista is one of southeast Calgary's most established and desirable addresses, with lake access, mature streets, and homes that routinely sell in around ten days. Buyers at this level notice finish, and the garage is part of the walkthrough. An original bay stacked with totes and tired shelving reads as unfinished; a clean coated floor and built-in cabinetry photograph as a real feature and signal a home that has been cared for right through to the garage. On a quick-moving Lake Bonavista listing, that is a quiet but genuine edge.

Frequently Asked Questions About Garage Organization in Lake Bonavista

Do you work on the original 1970s and 80s garages in Lake Bonavista?

Those are our specialty in Lake Bonavista. As Calgary's original lake community, most of the neighbourhood was built in the late 1960s through the 1980s, so the attached garages tend to be original single and double bays — often shallower and lower-ceilinged than a modern estate garage, sometimes with a tired slab and a sagging shelf or two nailed to the studs. That is exactly where a smart system pays off: we measure the real dimensions, then build up the walls and overhead so a compact original garage stores far more than it looks like it can, and the vehicle still parks inside. For the many homes that have been renovated or rebuilt, and for the larger Lake Bonavista Estates lots, we scale the same approach up to full custom cabinetry.

How do you store lake gear like paddleboards, kayaks and fishing kit for the lake?

The private 52-acre lake is the heart of Lake Bonavista, and it shows up in every garage: paddleboards, kayaks, canoes, life jackets, beach chairs, towels, and fishing gear for the summer, all fighting for the same corner. We build a proper lake wall — vertical racks and ceiling hoists for the big boards and boats, wall hooks for jackets and paddles, a drip-and-dry zone so wet gear is not soaking your cabinets, and labelled bins for the small stuff — so the whole family can grab what they need, walk down to the lake, and put it all back in minutes instead of climbing over a pile.

Can you store winter skating and hockey gear too?

Yes, and it is one of the things that makes Lake Bonavista garages unique. The lake freezes for skating and shinny, the community centre runs two indoor rinks, and Fish Creek's trails are right there, so the same garage that holds paddleboards in July holds skates, sticks, sleds, and toboggans in January. We plan the garage as a four-season space: a hooks-and-bins zone for skates and helmets by the door, a rack for sticks and shovels, and a simple summer-to-winter swap plan so the gear you are using is always the gear in reach, and the off-season fleet is up on the wall or overhead.

Is an epoxy or polyaspartic floor worth it on an older Lake Bonavista slab?

A slab that has been through fifty-plus Calgary winters of chinook freeze-thaw, road salt, and dripping wet gear is usually pitted, dusty, and stained. We grind it back, repair the cracks and spalled patches that come with age, then lay a polyaspartic or epoxy system that seals the concrete, brightens a dim older bay, and turns cleanup into a quick squeegee. Which areas do you serve around Lake Bonavista?

If you are near the lake, the community centre, Avenida, Macleod Trail, or the north edge of Fish Creek Provincial Park, you are squarely in our service area.
