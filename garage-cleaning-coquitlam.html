Why the Tri-Cities Have Their Own Garage Problem

Coquitlam, Port Moody and Port Coquitlam are family suburbs with mountains at the top of the street, and that combination produces a very specific garage.

The gear load

Tri-Cities garages carry more family sports and outdoor equipment per square foot than almost anywhere else we work. Two adults and two children means eight bikes across sizes as kids grow, four sets of ski or snowboard equipment, hockey bags, camping gear for two different kinds of trip, paddleboards, and the rack system for a car that's since been replaced. Almost none of it is used year-round and all of it is bulky.

The failure mode is predictable: it starts on the floor because there's nowhere else, the floor fills, the car goes outside, and within two years the garage is a storage room. Our answer is a seven-zone layout that puts seasonal gear overhead, active gear on the walls at accessible height, and defends the parking bay rather than treating it as leftover space.

Burke Mountain and elevation

North Coquitlam and Burke Mountain sit meaningfully higher than the valley floor, and they take more rain for it — the same orographic effect that soaks the North Shore, at smaller scale. Combined with heavy tree cover on the newer Burke Mountain developments, moss on aprons and door lines is noticeably worse up there than in central Coquitlam or Port Coquitlam. Sloped lots also mean more under-house and tuck-under garages, with the uphill-wall drainage issues that come with them.

Townhouse tandem density

The Tri-Cities carry a large volume of townhouse tandem garages, particularly around Burquitlam, Coquitlam Centre, Suter Brook and Klahanie. Long and narrow, single door, furnace and hot water tank inside, strata bylaws governing storage. Put a family gear load into a tandem garage and the back third becomes an unreachable pile within a year — which is the single most common Tri-Cities job we quote.

Older Maillardville and central PoCo

Maillardville is one of the oldest communities in the region, and central Port Coquitlam has substantial pre-war and post-war stock. Detached lane garages, original porous slabs, and the moisture and oil-staining that come with age. These get low-pressure methods rather than a full-pressure wash.

Size the gear zone for the kids you'll have in three years

The commonest design mistake in a Tri-Cities garage is sizing storage for today's bikes. Children's equipment turns over on a two-to-three year cycle and every size change leaves the old one behind. We size the gear zone with growth room and build in an obvious outbound route — a labelled 'outgrown' bin that goes to donation when it fills — because otherwise the garage silently accumulates a museum of bikes nobody can ride.

Garage Types We Work On in Coquitlam

Townhouse tandem garage

Burquitlam, Coquitlam Centre, Suter Brook, Klahanie, Fremont. Long, narrow, single door, mechanical equipment inside, strata rules in play. Narrow-depth shelving down one side, walking channel preserved, clearances held.

Newer Burke Mountain attached garage

Larger double garages on sloped, treed lots. Generally sound construction — the issues are moss, apron moisture and, above all, gear capacity.

Under-house and tuck-under garage

Common on north Coquitlam and Port Moody's sloped streets. One wall retaining wet ground, shared air with the house above. The uphill wall is where we look first.

Older Maillardville and PoCo lane garage

Pre-war and post-war detached structures, unheated, original slabs. Worst moisture and rodent exposure in the Tri-Cities, and the concrete usually won't take high pressure.

Disposal & Drive Times from Coquitlam

The Coquitlam Transfer Station on United Boulevard is the closest facility for most Tri-Cities jobs, which keeps runs short and drive time off your invoice. Non-divertible loads go on to the Vancouver Landfill in Delta.

Coquitlam sits in a good middle position — nothing like the North Shore's long haul, if not quite Surrey's doorstep advantage. On a typical Tri-Cities cleanout that means one or two short runs rather than an afternoon of driving.

We sort before hauling: reuse, donation, stewardship return, recycling, then landfill. That matters especially here because so much of a Tri-Cities garage is reusable sports equipment — bikes in particular have strong reuse channels in BC, and every diverted item is a tipping fee avoided. Confirm current facility hours and rates before hauling your own load; full 2026 detail is on our garage cleanout page.

Coquitlam Garage Services — FAQs

Do you serve all three Tri-Cities?

Yes — Coquitlam, Port Moody and Port Coquitlam, including Burke Mountain, Westwood Plateau, Burquitlam, Maillardville, Suter Brook, Klahanie, Citadel Heights and Mary Hill. We also cover Anmore and Belcarra, and neighbouring Burnaby and New Westminster.

How much does garage cleaning cost in Coquitlam?

A single-car or tandem garage clean starts at $249 and a standard double at $349. A full tandem declutter and organise typically runs $749 to $1,100. Cleanouts start at $399 plus disposal at cost. The Coquitlam Transfer Station on United Boulevard is close, which keeps disposal drive time reasonable.

How do you store gear for a family of four?

Vertically, and by season rather than by object type. Seasonal equipment — off-season skis, camping kit, holiday decorations — goes on overhead racking, because it's bulky, light and rarely accessed. Active gear goes on wall systems at accessible height. Bikes go on vertical hooks or ceiling hoists, never on the slab where the drivetrain corrodes. Camping gear gets grouped by trip type in sealed bins so packing is one lift instead of ten decisions.

Is Burke Mountain really wetter than the rest of Coquitlam?

Noticeably, yes. It's the same orographic effect that soaks the North Shore, at a smaller scale — higher ground forces air upward and it drops more of its moisture there. Combined with heavy tree cover on the newer Burke Mountain developments, moss on aprons and door lines is measurably worse up there than in central Coquitlam or Port Coquitlam.

Can you work in my townhouse tandem garage?

Yes, and it's a large share of our Tri-Cities work. Tandem garages are long and narrow with a single door and the furnace and hot water tank inside, so access is the constraint rather than dirt. We use narrow-depth shelving down one side only so the walking channel survives, hold code clearances around the mechanical equipment, and flag anything likely to breach your strata bylaws or the BC Fire Code.

What do we do with the kids' outgrown bikes?

Route them to reuse. Children's equipment turns over every two to three years and every size change leaves the old one behind, which is how a Tri-Cities garage quietly accumulates a museum of unridable bikes. BC has strong bike reuse channels, and every item diverted to donation avoids a tipping fee, so it lowers your bill as well. We build a labelled outgrown bin into the layout so there's an obvious outbound route rather than a pile.
