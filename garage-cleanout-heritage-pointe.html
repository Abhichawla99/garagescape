Garage Cleanout and Junk Removal in Heritage Pointe

In the Lake at Heritage Pointe, the community bylaws put three things in your garage: the garbage carts, any trailer staying longer than 72 hours, and any car parked for more than 48. When the garage is already full, something ends up outside and the owners association writes. We clear the garage, take every load to the right drop-off on either side of the city line, and leave nothing on the driveway.

Heritage Pointe sits just over Calgary's southern boundary, north of Dunbow Road between Deerfoot and Macleod Trail. It isn't part of the City. It's a hamlet in Foothills County, built from 1991 around the Heritage Pointe Golf Club, a private lake and the Pine Creek valley, and about 1,974 people lived in its 707 occupied homes at the 2021 census. The first houses are now around thirty years old, and so are some of the garages' contents.

Two things make a garage cleanout here different from one in Calgary. The houses inside the Lake at Heritage Pointe carry community bylaws, registered against every title, that rely on the garage for storage. And because the hamlet is in the County, the City's drop-offs and bulk rules don't map neatly onto your address. This page covers both, plus what we've planned for when a house here is being sold or cleared.

Five Bylaws That Make the Garage Your Storage Room

The Lake at Heritage Pointe Owners Association governs 490 homes. Its community bylaws were replaced in full at the 2017 annual meeting and are registered as an encumbrance and restrictive covenant on each homeowner's land title, so they bind you whether or not you've read them. Five sections decide how much your garage has to hold.

If the Association Has Already Written to You

Both fines in the table start only after a written warning, and they run per day until the problem is fixed. So the letter is a deadline, and the quickest fix is usually making room inside rather than paying for off-site storage month after month. Here's how we handle it.

  1. Send us the letter's date and what it's about. Carts, a trailer and a parked car each need a different amount of floor. We'll tell you the first day we can come and won't promise one we can't keep.
  2. Measure what has to go in. A trailer's overall length including the hitch, its height and its width, or the car's length with the mirrors folded. We check those against the empty bay before we call the job done.
  3. We clear the floor the item needs first. If the day runs long, the bay that solves the letter is empty and swept before we start on the shelves.
  4. You move the vehicle in. Our crew doesn't tow, back up trailers or drive clients' cars. We leave the bay ready for you.

If you'd rather have the garage organized afterwards so it stays that way, our Heritage Pointe garage organization page covers shelving and storage. That's a separate visit.

No Bulk Pickup: Where a Heritage Pointe Garage Actually Goes

Foothills County doesn't run garbage pickup for most residents. The landfill's own FAQ lists only Blackie, Cayley, Silvertip, Hawks Landing and Aldersyde as having it. In the Lake, the owners association brought in its own Universal Cart Program in January 2017: black, blue and green carts, collected curbside, with lids that have to close. Bagged waste left beside a cart isn't collected, and the association recommends clear bags because loads can be inspected where they're delivered. That's built for a household's weekly garbage. A garage's worth goes elsewhere.

What a Thirty-Year-Old Garage Here Tends to Hold

An example of a long-held garage, not a photo of a Heritage Pointe client's home. Most of what's in it splits across four or five different drop-offs.

Heritage Pointe's houses were built over a long stretch, from the early 1990s phases near the golf course to newer areas approved since, so the garages differ. In the older streets we plan for the things that pile up when one family has used a garage for decades:

  • Old paint and stain from the original build and every touch-up since. Free at the Foothills hazardous waste area, but it has to be separated from the garbage.
  • Gas mowers, trimmers, fuel cans and propane cylinders. We set fuel aside and keep cylinders upright and apart from the main load.
  • A second fridge or chest freezer. $20 each for Freon at Foothills. Empty it a day ahead so it isn't full of thawing food when we arrive.
  • Old stereos, VCRs and DVD players. The Foothills e-waste program doesn't take them, so they're a separate stop.
  • Lake and golf gear. Kayaks, paddles, life jackets and sets of clubs that nobody uses any more. Good ones go to donation or to a family member; worn-out foam and broken hulls go to landfill.
  • Garden chemicals and empty containers. Unused product goes to hazardous waste. Empty pesticide containers are accepted free only if they've been triple-rinsed.
  • Boxes that came out of the house and never went back. These take the longest, because only you can decide on them.

If boxes have been stacked for years against an outside wall, check them for mice before you start lifting. Our acreage cleanout page covers how we handle droppings safely, and the same steps apply to an attached garage near the Pine Creek valley.

One Truck, Nothing Left on the Driveway

Under section 2.1.6, materials can't be stored on the lot, and a skip bin parked on the driveway for a week sits awkwardly with that. If you want a rented bin, ask the owners association first. We work the other way: the truck arrives with the crew, loads as we sort, and leaves with everything the same day. Anything still waiting for your decision goes back inside the garage, not onto the drive.

The truck parks on the street in front of your house, never across a neighbour's driveway, and the load is covered before it leaves, which the law requires on a public road anyway. On garbage day we work around the carts so the collection truck can still reach them.

You don't need to be home on the day. Walk us through the garage in person or on a video call, give us the overhead door code, and we'll put anything that looks personal or valuable in a set-aside pile for you, then send photos when we're done.

Questions From Heritage Pointe Homeowners

What is a garage cleanout?

A garage cleanout empties the garage of everything you don't want to keep and takes it away the same day. In Heritage Pointe that means sorting each item to where it can legally go: donation, the Foothills Regional Landfill's free recycling and household hazardous waste area, the paid landfill, or a Calgary drop-off. We finish by sweeping the floor so the carts, the cars or the trailer can go back in.

Can I park a trailer or RV on my driveway in Heritage Pointe?

Not for long. The Lake at Heritage Pointe community bylaws (section 2.1.4) say recreational vehicles, trailers and oversize vehicles can't be kept on a lot or the street beside it for more than 72 hours unless they are fully inside the garage or the owners association has given written approval. After a written warning, the fine is $100 a day. Section 2.1.7 adds that any vehicle stored for more than 48 hours has to be fully inside a garage. Other parts of the hamlet, such as Artesia, have their own covenants, so check yours.

How much does junk removal cost in Heritage Pointe?

We quote from photos or a visit. The main factors are how much goes to paid disposal by weight, items with their own fees, how many separate drop-offs the load needs, and how much sorting needs you there. For reference, the Foothills Regional Landfill charges a $10 minimum for loads up to 100 kg and surcharges heavier loads, adds $20 for each Freon appliance, and takes tires without rims, used oil, antifreeze, paint and household hazardous waste free. City of Calgary landfills charge $113 a tonne for garbage in 2026, with a $25 flat fee under 250 kg.

Where can I dump garbage near Heritage Pointe?

Foothills County has two transfer stations, Priddis (Tuesday and Saturday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m.) and Diamond Valley (Friday and Saturday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m.), but they take bagged household garbage only, in $2 County bags. Anything bigger goes to the Foothills Regional Landfill on Highway 783, open Monday to Saturday 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., or to a City of Calgary landfill. Loads on a public road must be tarped or tied down; the Foothills landfill adds a $10 surcharge for an unsecured load.

How do you properly clean out a garage?

Start with what the garage has to hold when you're done: in the Lake at Heritage Pointe that's at least the three carts, plus any car or trailer that would otherwise sit outside longer than the bylaws allow. Empty one wall at a time onto tarps, sort by where each item is going rather than by what it is, set aside paint, fuel and chemicals for the hazardous waste area, and haul everything the same day. Don't leave a pile on the driveway: the community bylaws don't allow materials stored on the lot.

Will you take our old garbage carts?

No. In the Lake at Heritage Pointe, the roll carts are barcoded to the house address and stay with that address when the house is sold. We clean around them and make room for them inside the garage, which is where the bylaws say they have to be stored.

Do I need to be home for the cleanout?

Only for the walkthrough, which can be in person or on a video call. On the day we can work from the overhead door code, put anything that looks personal or valuable in a set-aside pile for you, and send photos when we finish.

What won't you take?

Ammunition, explosives and anything radioactive. The Foothills Regional Landfill can't accept them, and ammunition belongs with police or the RCMP. We also leave firearms, unlabelled chemicals we can't identify, and the carts that belong to the house. Everything else in a typical garage, including fridges, tires, paint, propane cylinders and old electronics, we sort and take.
