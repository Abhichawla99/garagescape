Why Calgary Garages Get Filthier Than Most

A garage in a mild climate collects dust and cobwebs. A Calgary garage collects a chemistry experiment. Four things about living on the edge of the Rockies work against your concrete every winter — understanding them is why our deep clean is different from a quick sweep-and-hose.

Road Salt and the "Pickle" Mix

The City of Calgary and Alberta Transportation keep winter routes drivable with a sand-and-salt "pickle" mix and, on priority roads, liquid anti-icing brines. Every trip home, your tires and undercarriage carry that slush onto the slab, where it dries into a grey-white crust of salt and grit. It is corrosive to concrete, to steel shelving legs, and to the underside of the vehicle parked on top of it. Sweeping barely touches it — salt bonds into the pores and needs to be dissolved and flushed out, not brushed around.

Chinooks and the Freeze-Thaw Grinder

Calgary sees more chinooks than almost anywhere in Canada — warm winds that can lift the temperature 20 degrees in an afternoon. Snow and ice packed in your wheel wells melt onto the floor, then refreeze hard when the cold snaps back overnight. That melt-and-refreeze cycle, repeated dozens of times a winter, drives salty water deep into the concrete and then expands it from the inside. The visible result is pitting, flaking (spalling) and a slab that looks a decade older than it is. A thorough clean before spring is the cheapest damage control there is.

Efflorescence — the White Bloom

If you have seen a chalky white haze creep across your garage floor, that is efflorescence: minerals inside the concrete dissolving in all that meltwater and wicking to the surface as the slab dries out. It is extremely common on Calgary slabs because of how much moisture and salt they take on over winter. Scrubbing it with a wire brush just grinds it in. We remove it with the correct concrete cleaner and can recommend sealing to slow how fast it returns.

Gravel Dust, Oil and Summer Pests

Calgary's dry, windy summers and the city's many gravel back-lanes push a fine grit under every garage door, so the floor is never truly clean for long. Cold winters make it worse in another way: rubber seals stiffen below about minus twenty, so older vehicles drip more oil and coolant in January than they ever do in July. Add the wasp nests that appear in the eaves by August and the pigeons that find an open overhead door, and a Calgary garage accumulates a very specific mix of grime that a general house-cleaning crew is not equipped for. The Calgary Garage-Cleaning Calendar

Timing matters more here than in most cities, because the dirt is seasonal. Cleaning at the wrong moment means doing it again a month later. This is the rhythm we recommend to Calgary homeowners.

Late April to May — The Big Spring Clean

This is the most important clean of the year. By late spring the City has stopped sanding roads, the last snow has finally come off your vehicles, and a full winter of salt, grit and meltwater grime is sitting on the floor. Clearing it now stops the salt from doing its slow damage through the warm months and resets the slab before you spend the summer using the space. If you book one garage cleaning a year in Calgary, make it this one.

June to August — Summer Reset and Coating Prep

Warm, dry weather is the only reliable window to seal or coat a Calgary garage floor, because concrete needs to be clean and fully dry for anything to bond. If an epoxy or polyaspartic floor is on your list, the deep clean is step one and summer is the season for it. It is also the easiest time to pair cleaning with an organization project while the doors can stay open.

September to October — Beat the Freeze

A lighter clean before the snow tires and the road salt come back protects the floor through the hardest months. Clearing summer gravel dust, checking that drains and door seals are clear, and knocking down the season's wasp nests means winter slush lands on a clean, sound surface instead of grinding into last year's buildup.

Winter — Damage Control Between Storms

We do not recommend a full wet deep-clean in deep cold — water and freezing slabs do not mix — but a chinook thaw is a good moment to shovel out slush and let the floor dry before the next freeze. For estate and heated garages that stay above freezing, mid-winter maintenance cleans are perfectly workable and keep salt from ever building up in the first place.

Frequently Asked Questions About Garage Cleaning in Calgary

How long does garage cleaning take in Calgary?

Most garage cleaning projects in Calgary take 2-6 hours, depending on the size of your garage and the level of dirt and stains. A double garage with a season of road-salt and gravel buildup usually lands in the middle of that range. We'll provide a time estimate during your free consultation.

Why is Calgary road salt so damaging to a garage floor?

Calgary and Alberta Transportation treat winter roads with a sand-and-salt "pickle" mix and liquid anti-icing brines. Your tires and undercarriage carry that slush onto the slab, where chinook melt-and-refreeze cycles drive the salty water deep into the concrete pores — causing pitting, spalling and the white bloom called efflorescence. Removing the salt before spring is the single most protective thing you can do for a Calgary garage floor.

When is the best time of year to deep-clean a garage in Calgary?

Late April through May is ideal, once the City stops sanding roads and the last snow has come off your vehicles. That spring clean clears a full winter of salt, grit and meltwater grime. A second lighter clean in September or October, before de-icer season returns, keeps the floor protected through winter.

What is the white chalky residue on my garage floor?

That white powder is efflorescence — soluble minerals inside the concrete dissolving in moisture and migrating to the surface as the slab dries. Calgary's freeze-thaw swings and road-salt exposure accelerate it. We remove it with the correct concrete cleaner rather than a wire brush, and can recommend sealing to slow its return.

Can you remove oil stains from my Calgary garage floor?

Yes. Oil and automotive fluids leak more freely in Calgary's deep cold as seals stiffen, so winter drips are common. We lift stubborn oil, antifreeze and transmission-fluid marks with professional degreasers and hot-water pressure washing rather than store-bought products that only mask them.

Can you clean an epoxy-coated or polished garage floor?

Yes. Coated and showroom floors in estate garages need pH-neutral cleaners and controlled pressure so the finish isn't dulled or delaminated. We adjust our method to the coating and can safely maintain epoxy, polyaspartic and polished-concrete floors.

Do I need to move my car out of the garage?

Yes, we'll need access to the entire garage floor for thorough cleaning. We can help coordinate moving your vehicles to ensure the best cleaning results.

Will the cleaning products damage my garage floor?

No, we use professional-grade, floor-safe cleaning products designed for concrete and coated garage floor surfaces. We never use harsh chemicals that could cause damage.

How often should I have my Calgary garage professionally cleaned?

We recommend professional garage cleaning every 6-12 months, ideally on a spring-and-fall rhythm that matches Calgary's salt season. Heavy-use garages such as workshops or homes with multiple vehicles may need more frequent cleaning.
