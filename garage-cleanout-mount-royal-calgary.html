Lane, Parking Pad or Through the House?

Every cleanout in these neighbourhoods starts with one question: where does the truck stand, and how far do we carry? There are three answers, and the first one is the one we want.

What We Look at in Your Lane Before We Book

The photos you send us are for this. Any of these can slow a job down or change which truck we send, so we'd rather know before the truck arrives than when it's stuck.

  1. Width at your door, not at the lane entrance. A lane that's fine at the street can pinch down behind a neighbour's fence, bin pad or retaining wall.
  2. Overhead wires and branches. Many inner-city lanes carry power and telephone lines on poles, and mature trees hang low over them. A box truck needs clearance the whole way in, not just at your garage.
  3. What the lane is made of. Gravel lanes rut after a chinook thaw and stay soft for days. If yours is soft, we book for a cold morning or use a smaller truck. In winter, lanes often aren't ploughed, and the snow decides whether a truck gets to your door at all.
  4. Which door actually opens. Some old garages have an overhead door that hasn't moved in years and a man door on the side. We need to know which one the boxes come out of.
  5. Construction next door. Infill rebuilds happen on these streets too. A neighbour's bin, crane or fenced site can close the lane for weeks, and that changes the route.
  6. Your collection day. If your black, blue and green carts go out in the lane, we avoid booking the morning the trucks come through.

A Bin in the Lane Needs a City Permit

Some homeowners plan to rent a roll-off bin, fill it over a few weekends and have it collected. In a back lane, that needs paperwork. The City of Calgary's street use permit is required for exclusive use of any road right-of-way, and the City lists back lanes, alleys and "placement of a garbage bin" by name. In 2026 a site-specific permit is $31.50 a day plus GST, applied for through myID, and lower-impact closures need at least five business days' notice. A bin that blocks a lane through rush hour or for more than 24 hours can also need a hoarding permit.

A bin on your own parking pad or driveway avoids all of that. If you don't have one, a truck that loads in the lane and leaves the same afternoon is usually simpler than a bin that sits there for a week while your neighbours drive around it. It also means the load is sorted before it goes anywhere. A bin is usually charged as one mixed load, so the paint cans and the donatable bikes end up in the same price as the garbage.

Elbow Park After 2013: The Boxes Nobody Opened

Elbow Park is bounded by the Elbow River on the south and east, and in June 2013 the river came into it. Basements and garages flooded, and the province bought seventeen badly damaged Elbow Park homes, which were slated for demolition in 2016. In 2018 a University of Calgary study blamed groundwater flooding, rather than sewer backup, for damaging homes along the Elbow River that year. Roxboro and Rideau Park sit on the same river.

More than a decade later, we still find the leftovers of that summer in these garages: boxes carried out of a wet basement, set on the garage floor to dry, and never opened again. Here's how we deal with them.

  • Cardboard with a tide line goes out as it is. We open it outside, in the lane or on the pad, so whatever's grown inside doesn't spread through the garage.
  • Paper, photos and albums get looked at before they're binned. Some families want water-damaged photos kept even when they're past saving. That's your call, so we set them aside and ask.
  • Mould on stored furniture or a smell of must means gloves and masks for our crew. If there's visible mould on the garage structure itself (drywall, sheathing, the sill plate), we tell you, and that's work for a remediation contractor, not a cleanout crew.
  • The slab gets swept and looked at. Old water lines and efflorescence on the concrete are worth a photo for your records, and our garage floor cleaning service can deal with them afterwards.

When You Can't Be There

Plenty of people who call us from these neighbourhoods aren't in the house on the day: they're at work downtown, away for part of the winter, or managing the house for a parent from another city. That's fine. Here's how a cleanout runs when you're not home.

  1. A walkthrough first. In person or on a video call, room by room through the garage. You point at what stays; we write it down and take photos of it.
  2. A keep list we both have. We email it back to you before the day so there's no confusion about the bike in the corner or the box marked "Dad".
  3. Access from the outside. A garage door code, a lockbox key for the man door, or a neighbour who opens it. We don't need to go into the house.
  4. A pause for anything personal. If we open a box of letters, a jewellery case or a firearm case, we stop, photograph it, set it aside and message you. We don't decide those for you.
  5. Photos at the end. The empty garage, the swept floor, and the pile of things you asked us to keep.

Why Two Lane Garages Get Two Different Quotes

Two garages on the same block, the same size, can get very different quotes from us. These are the reasons, roughly in order of how much they move the price.

  • Weight to landfill. The City charges by the tonne. A garage full of old furniture and rugs weighs more and costs more to dispose of than one full of bikes and garden tools that can be donated or recycled.
  • The carry. A truck backed up to the garage door loads far faster than one parked on the street with every box carried through a side yard and down the steps.
  • How much needs a decision. A garage you've already sorted goes quickly. A garage where every box has to be opened and shown to you takes longer, and we'd rather quote for that honestly than rush it.
  • Hazardous waste. Free to drop off, but the City's 20-litre limit per visit means a garage with forty old paint cans needs more than one trip.
  • Mice and water damage. Both slow the work down, because both mean protective gear and opening boxes outside.

Photos of the lane and the inside of the garage get you a quote without a visit. For a big job, or one where most of it needs sorting with you, we'll come and look. Our main Calgary garage cleanout page explains what's free to dispose of in the city and how sorting reduces the bill.

Questions From Mount Royal and Elbow Park Homeowners

What is a garage cleanout?

A cleanout empties the garage of what you no longer want: we carry it out, sort it into donation, recycling, household hazardous waste and landfill, haul it away and sweep the floor. It's different from a garage cleaning, which is the floor and walls, and from organization, which is shelving and storage. In an older Mount Royal or Elbow Park garage, the cleanout usually comes first and the other two follow if you want them.

How much does a garage cleanout cost in Mount Royal or Elbow Park?

We quote after seeing photos or the garage itself, because three things move the price more than size: how much has to go to landfill by weight, how far we carry it to the truck, and how much needs sorting with you. City of Calgary landfill rates in 2026 are $113 a tonne for garbage, with a $25 flat fee for loads under 250 kg; paint and chemicals go free to household hazardous waste drop-offs, up to 20 litres per visit. A lane garage the truck can back up to costs less to clear than the same garage reached through a side yard.

Do I need a permit to put a bin in my back lane in Calgary?

Yes. The City of Calgary requires a street use permit to use any road right-of-way exclusively, and it names back lanes, alleys and the placement of a garbage bin specifically. The permit is applied for through myID, costs $31.50 a day plus GST for a site-specific permit, and lower-impact closures need at least five business days' notice. A bin on your own parking pad or driveway is on private property and doesn't use the lane.

How do I clear out an old detached garage?

Start with what can hurt you: open the doors, look for mouse droppings before you move anything, and pull out paint, fuel and garden chemicals so they don't end up in a garbage load. Then sort by destination rather than by room corner: donate, City hazardous waste, recycling, landfill and keep. Open every box before it goes, because older garages in these neighbourhoods often hold paperwork, photos or tools that belonged to a previous owner or a parent. Haul last, and sweep once the slab is bare.

Can you clean out the garage while I'm away?

Yes. We walk through it with you first, in person or on a video call, and agree on what stays. On the day we work from the lane door or the overhead door with the code you give us, set aside anything that looks valuable or personal instead of deciding for you, and send photos when the job is done.

Will paint from an old garage wall come off during the cleanout?

We don't scrape or sand walls. Health Canada says a home built before 1960 probably contains lead-based paint, and one built between 1960 and 1990 may have it on exterior surfaces. Many garages in Mount Royal and Elbow Park are that old. If the paint is flaking onto stored boxes, we bag the flakes with the debris and leave the walls alone; repainting or abatement is a job for a painter who works with lead.

Where can seniors in Calgary get free hoarding help?

Sage Seniors Association runs a program called This Full House for people 55 and over, and Elements Calgary has a decluttering program for adults. The City's Home Services for Seniors program closed to new clients on September 30, 2026; the Government of Alberta's Special Needs Assistance for Seniors is reached through Alberta Supports at 1-877-644-9992. If the garage is part of a bigger hoarding situation, our hoarding garage cleanup page explains how we handle it.
