Garage Cleanout and Junk Removal in Edgemont and Varsity Estates

Edgemont climbs the side of Nose Hill and Varsity Estates backs onto Bowmont Park. Both are northwest Calgary neighbourhoods of front-drive houses where the garage is the only way in or out for everything you own. We clear them for a sale, a downsizing move or just so the car goes back inside before January, and we plan around the slope, the park behind the fence and this month's water restrictions.

Edgemont started building in 1978. Its streets run from Edgemont Estates, the oldest part, through Edgedale, Edenwold, Edgepark, Edgevalley and Edgebrook to Edgeridge, the newest. The City's 2012 census counted about 5,400 homes there. A house on one of the first streets has had the same double garage for more than 45 years, and in a lot of cases the same owners.

Varsity Estates is the upscale corner of Varsity, south of Crowchild Trail and west toward the Bow. It's almost all single-detached houses with no rear lanes, and much of it sits against Bowmont Natural Park or the Silver Springs Golf and Country Club. Varsity as a whole went up in the 1960s and early 1970s, so the garages here are older still.

November 2026: No Hose, No Pressure Washer

On November 4, 2026 the City put Calgary under Stage 4 outdoor water restrictions while crews connect the new Bearspaw South Feeder Main. The City expects the old main to be shut down for about four weeks. Under Stage 4 nobody may wash a driveway, sidewalk or walkway, and the City is asking building operators to put off even indoor parkade cleaning until the restrictions lift. Fines start at $3,000.

A garage floor wash runs straight out the door and down the driveway, so for now we don't do one. A cleanout doesn't need water, though. Here's how we split the job while the restrictions are on:

Loading Off a Sloped Driveway in November

Much of Edgemont is the northwest end of Nose Hill. The southeast corner of the community is steep escarpment, the highest ground sits around 1,245 metres, and that's why so many Edgemont houses get the mountain view. It also means a lot of driveways that tilt down to the street, some of them facing north and sitting in shade from November to March.

A cleanout is a few hundred trips between the garage and the truck. On a level driveway that's just walking. On a slope that's had a chinook melt in the afternoon and a freeze overnight, it's where a dresser or a chest freezer gets away from someone. Before we book, we look at the street photo and decide:

  • Whether the truck parks at the bottom and we carry down, or backs up the driveway so the load goes level into the box. That depends on driveway length and how steep the last few metres are.
  • Whether the job needs a morning start so the sunny side has thawed, or an early one before it melts and refreezes into a glaze.
  • Which items go on a dolly with straps and which get carried by two people. Freezers, safes, gun-cabinet-sized steel cabinets and loaded workbenches don't roll down a hill.
  • Whether to sand or scrape the walking line first. If the driveway is glazed when we arrive and it isn't safe, we reschedule rather than risk it.

Varsity Estates is flatter, on the plateau above the Bow valley, but it has the other problem. With no lanes, the front driveway is the only route, and on a cul-de-sac the truck may have to share the bulb with the neighbours' cars. If you know your neighbours' schedules, a heads-up the day before saves everyone a blocked driveway.

When the Park Is Behind Your Fence

Backing onto Bowmont Natural Park or onto Nose Hill means coyotes, and a cleanout leaves the overhead door open for hours. In a 2026 coyote notice for a northwest Calgary community, the City told residents to secure anything that draws wildlife, naming pet food, garbage, compost, fallen fruit and bird seed. Most of those live in a garage.

What 45 Years in One Garage Looks Like

A garage that's been with one family since the late 1970s or early 1980s fills in layers. The oldest layer is usually against the back wall and up in the rafters, and it's the part people have stopped seeing. In Edgemont and Varsity Estates that tends to mean:

  • Paint from when the house was new. Cans of the original colours, often rusted shut. These go free to a City household hazardous waste drop-off, and Varsity has one at its own fire station (see the map below).
  • The kids' things. Hockey bags, a sled collection, school projects, boxes labelled with names of people who now live in Toronto. These need a family decision, so they go in their own pile and we photograph them for whoever isn't there.
  • Retired electronics. Tube TVs, stereo cabinets, old computers. Free at a City Eco Centre as long as they arrive separate from charged garbage.
  • A second freezer or beer fridge that may or may not still work. We check before you decide.
  • Garden and golf gear from decades of yard work and, in Varsity Estates, often a short walk to Silver Springs. Usable clubs and tools go to donation; broken ones go as scrap metal or garbage.

If it's for a sale, the cleanout before selling page works back from possession day.

What Moves the Quote

We price each garage from the photos or a walkthrough. We don't have a flat rate for Edgemont or Varsity. These are the things that change the number:

  • Weight at the landfill scale. A garage of paper and boxes is light. Concrete pavers, old tile, a cast-iron woodstove or a basement's worth of books is not.
  • How much sorts out free. Paint, electronics and tires that go to their own stream don't cost a tipping fee.
  • Refrigerant appliances. Each fridge, freezer or air conditioner has its own City charge on top of its weight.
  • The driveway. A steep or icy carry takes more people or more time than a flat one.
  • Decisions. If the family wants to go box by box, the job takes longer than one where you tell us "keep the bikes, everything else goes."
  • Timing. A fixed possession date or an estate deadline sets the schedule. Tell us the date with the photos.

Questions From Edgemont and Varsity Estates Homeowners

How much does it cost to remove junk from a garage in Calgary?

We quote from photos or a walkthrough, not a flat price. The biggest factors are how much weight ends up on a paid landfill scale, how much of the garage can go to free streams instead, fridges and freezers with their own fees, and how hard the carry is from the garage to the truck. For reference, the City's 2026 landfill rates are $25 flat for a load under 250 kg, $113 a tonne for garbage above that, and $17 per refrigerant appliance plus its weight.

How do I get rid of an old couch from the garage in Calgary?

If it's clean and in usable shape, a donation or resale is the cheapest route. If it's stained, torn or has been sitting in a garage with mice, it goes to the landfill as garbage by weight. It won't go in the black cart, because the City doesn't collect items longer than 1.25 metres or heavier than 20 kg, and the community cleanup events for 2026 are finished. We take couches as part of a garage cleanout.

What is the cheapest way to get rid of junk from an Edgemont or Varsity garage?

Separate it first. Paint, solvents and fuel go free to the household hazardous waste drop-off at Varsity Fire Station #17, 3400 37 Street NW, open 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. every day. Electronics, tires and textiles are free at the Spyhill Eco Centre if they arrive on their own. Only the garbage that's left costs anything at the scale. Doing the runs yourself is cheaper than hiring a crew; hiring saves the trips, the lifting and the truck.

Can you pressure wash my garage floor in November 2026?

Not while Calgary is under Stage 4 water restrictions, which started November 4, 2026. The City prohibits washing driveways, sidewalks and walkways during Stage 4, with fines starting at $3,000, and has asked building operators to postpone even indoor parkade cleaning. We clear and dry-sweep the garage now and can come back to wash the floor once the City lifts the restrictions, which it expects after about four weeks.

How do you tackle a garage that's been full for decades?

From the door inward. We clear a working area in the driveway, pull out anything that attracts wildlife or is hazardous, then work back to the rear wall and up into the rafters. Everything gets sorted into keep, family decision, donate, hazardous, free-at-the-landfill and garbage before it's loaded, so nothing is paid for that didn't need to be.

Is it safe to leave the garage open all day if we back onto Nose Hill or Bowmont Park?

It's fine with someone working in it, but it's the time to remove what draws coyotes and other animals. The City lists pet food, garbage, compost, fallen fruit and bird seed as attractants to secure. We move those first, keep the door to the house closed if you have pets, and close the overhead door when we leave.

Do I need to be home for the cleanout?

Only for the walkthrough, and that can be a video call with family members who live elsewhere. On the day we can work from the door code. Anything that looks personal, valuable or irreplaceable goes in a set-aside pile, and we send photos of it and of the finished garage.
