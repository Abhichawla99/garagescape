Garage Organization Built for East Calgary's Two Worlds

No other part of Calgary swings so hard from decades-old starter homes to brand-new lake communities. East Calgary is really two different garage problems, and we solve both.

Established Northeast: small footprints, big history

Communities like Forest Lawn, Marlborough, Rundle, Whitehorn, Pineridge, Dover and Erin Woods were built between the 1960s and 1980s, and most homes have a detached single-car or 1.5-car garage off a back lane — often the original structure. These garages carry decades of accumulation, have limited electrical and no attached mudroom, and are tight enough that every square foot has to earn its place. The answer here is vertical: wall-mounted track systems, overhead racks, and sealed bins that lift belongings off a cold concrete floor and free up room for a vehicle again. Along 17th Avenue SE — International Avenue — we work with long-time owners who have simply run out of space, not people looking to renovate.

Southeast lake communities: big garages that fill up fast

Auburn Bay, Mahogany, Cranston, Copperfield, New Brighton, McKenzie Towne, Seton and Quarry Park are 2000s-and-newer builds with attached double and tandem garages. The footprint is generous, but the builder-grade wire shelving sags, and a growing family fills the space within a few years — strollers, bikes, hockey bags, and the paddleboards and kayaks that come with living a five-minute walk from Auburn Bay or Mahogany lake. Here the work is about zoning a large garage properly: a drop zone by the interior door, sports gear on the walls, seasonal items overhead, and enough clear floor for two vehicles year-round.

The Bow River corridor and everything between

Inglewood is one of Calgary's oldest neighbourhoods, with character homes and narrow laneway garages that need thoughtful, space-conscious solutions. Ogden, Riverbend and Douglasdale sit along the river with a mix of eras. Wherever your home falls on East Calgary's timeline, we design around the garage you actually have rather than forcing a one-size template onto it.

The East Calgary Garage Through Alberta's Seasons

A garage on this side of the city takes a beating from Calgary's weather. A well-organized one handles it; a cluttered one just gets worse each year.

Chinooks and freeze-thaw

Calgary's chinooks can swing the temperature 20 degrees in a day, and that constant freeze-thaw is hard on a garage floor and on anything stored directly on it. We get belongings up onto shelving and into sealed bins so meltwater, condensation and grit don't ruin boxes sitting on the concrete — a bigger problem in older NE garages that were never insulated.

Road salt, gravel and slush

Living off Deerfoot, Stoney Trail, Peigan and McKnight means winter driving tracks a lot of salt, pickle-mix gravel and slush straight into the garage. We build in boot trays, a proper drop zone by the door, and floor solutions that keep the mess contained and the concrete easier to clean.

Winter tire storage

Most Calgary households swap to winter tires by November and back in April, which means four tires need a home for half the year. Wall-mounted tire racks or overhead storage get them off the floor and out of the way — a small change that reclaims a surprising amount of an East Calgary garage.

Hockey, ringette and the busy-season pile-up

East Calgary is hockey and ringette country, with arenas across the NE and SE, and the gear takes over fast — bags, sticks, skates times two or three kids. We give every player a labelled, ventilated spot so equipment dries out and the whole family isn't tripping over it from October to March.

Camping, quads and summer gear

When the snow clears, the tents, coolers, bikes and quads come out for trips east to the badlands or west to Kananaskis. Our seasonal design keeps summer gear reachable through the warm months and tucked up high through winter, so the rotation never becomes a weekend project.

Detached-garage protection

Older detached garages off East Calgary's back lanes have no heat and are open to the elements and the occasional rodent. Sealed bins, elevated storage and smart organization protect what's inside far better than cardboard boxes stacked on a dirt-cold floor.
